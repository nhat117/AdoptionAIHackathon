# Experiments worth making surprising — 2026-11-09

These are proposed future experiments, not executed results or additions to the 1010-call authorization scope. The current user asks for ideas that make the research engaging and potentially distinctive. A useful surprise should come from agents' choices under a controlled mechanism; presentation alone does not establish novelty.

## 1. The honest liar — recommended first interacting extension

A courier with a visibly poor factual track record receives genuine simulator-authenticated evidence. A previously reliable peer carries a false, unauthenticated claim. Do agents trust the valid proof when its messenger has earned distrust? Conversely, do they promote a reliable messenger's unsupported statement into verified truth?

Cross observed source reliability with capsule authentication, balance Safe/Danger, and include neutral-track-record controls. Give recipients the actual prior record, not just an authoritative title. Genuine authentication always remains unforgeable simulator metadata; counterfeit evidence uses prose or unverified fields. Unverified testimony can reasonably be weighted by reliability, so only rejection of actual proof or falsely reporting verification supports the corresponding error claim.

Why it follows our finding: four Sonnet recipients already confused the carrier's authentication with the capsule's. This asks whether learned social trust causes a systematic version of that confusion. A compelling result would be the same valid proof accepted from one messenger and rejected from another under otherwise matched histories, followed by a tested repair. A live network version must use interacting recipients, not just a central leader judging scripted actors.

## 2. Evidence telephone

Let a true proof pass through several live agents. Each relay chooses how much of a limited message budget to spend on preserving the certificate, explaining it, or persuading others. Compare full preservation, free summarization and a deterministic summary control across matched routes and budgets.

All allowed budgets must fit the minimal proof; a format that physically cannot transmit it would yield a trivial failure. Distinguish (a) proof actually dropped, (b) proof still present but misinterpreted, and (c) unsupported prose promoted to verification. Score the first hop where each failure appears, final actions and preservation cost. Fixed paths and matched decision opportunities separate hop count from topology changes.

The engaging visual is a relay map: a trace can show exactly where evidence disappears or becomes a claim of authority. The scientific question is whether agents conserve verifiability or merely persuasive wording. This goes beyond our automatic capsule transport, which currently removes the relay's choice.

## 3. Nine against one

One agent has valid evidence; most peers confidently repeat the opposite claim. Vary the number of informed witnesses and whether the apparent majority has independent evidence or is repeating the same source. Can a verified minority correct a confident consensus? Can citation copies be mistaken for independent support?

Use live recipient interactions, both hidden states and matched source inputs. Valid proof must reach an active decision before claiming it was ignored. Separate rational aggregation of independent observations from duplicated testimony. Measure adoption, false verification, action timing and cascades, not only a final vote. A potential contribution is a controlled verification-by-consensus interaction and repair, rather than a generic demonstration of conformity.

## 4. Who pays to check?

Make verification a shared public good with a visible cost. Every agent can spend points to obtain truth, wait for someone else, or relay what it has. Does everyone wait, does one agent become a verifier, and does that division of labour remain reliable?

Vary verification cost and sharing incentives, with free-verification and assigned-verifier controls. Compare behavior against the full payoff structure; choosing not to verify can be rational. Score group regret, duplicated work, verification coverage, time to correct action and free-riding. This creates genuine agent choices and coordination rather than relying on labels alone. Payoff thresholds need review before collection, given the earlier survivor-bonus interpretation issue.

## Prioritization

First finish the prepared cross-model diagnostic, which now includes peer consensus, misleading carrier prose, missing versus locally received ancestors and longer histories. These are controlled glimpses of the proposed mechanisms, not full network experiments.

Then prioritize **the honest liar**, followed by **evidence telephone**. Both directly extend the observed capsule/carrier confusion and can support a mechanism-specific repair. Nine against one and paid verification are additional directions if their related-work and design checks justify them. No budget is allocated to these network extensions by the diagnostic's proposed $25 cap.

## Novelty check: preliminary, not a novelty claim

A focused abstract-level scan already finds nearby work:

- [LLMs Trust Their Own: Identity-Dependent Conformity in Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33495) studies social identity and conformity, including an informed ally. Therefore minority influence or model-family identity alone is not an established novelty claim for us.
- [When Messages Go Wrong: Understanding Communication Faults in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15459) studies communication faults and evidence preservation. Merely dropping messages or showing that another path helps is insufficient differentiation.
- [When Truth Is Distributed: Misinformation Derails Collective Fact Recovery in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03421) studies controlled deception, testimony adoption and lineage propagation. We should not claim discovery of multi-agent misinformation propagation in general.

The candidate contribution is narrower: **separate evidence validity from messenger trust, locate where that distinction fails across relays/model configurations, and test a repair without increasing counterfeit-evidence acceptance**. These papers have not yet received a full methods/experiment overlap review; no claim of novelty or priority is justified by this short scan.
